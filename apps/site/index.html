An AI assistant for NGO back-office work, on your own computer

NGOmate is free and open source. It runs on an open-weight model on your computer, so there is no subscription to pay, and what you type stays with you unless you connect something else.

Why it exists

Many small organizations cannot pay for an AI subscription for everyone on the team, or do not want their documents on someone else's servers. NGOmate does the everyday work of finding funding, drafting and reporting with a model small enough for an ordinary laptop. No hosted AI service is needed, and you decide what, if anything, leaves your computer.

What it does

  • Chat with a local model

    The model is a separate download. You can also point NGOmate at a model server you run.

  • Draft from your documents

    It reads the PDF, Word and text files you give it and drafts from them.

  • Memory you can read

    Memory is kept as plain Markdown files you can read, edit and delete. It asks before saving anything.

  • Connect your other tools

    Connectors use the open Model Context Protocol. Anything that changes something asks you first.

  • Ready-made tasks

    Find funding opportunities, draft a proposal section, or write an update or report.

Where it's going

We want every small organization to have an AI colleague of its own: one that knows your rules, your funders and your past work, takes the paperwork off your desk, and never sends you a bill.

  • Next

    Follows your AI-use policy

    Pick an Open, Standard or Strict policy and NGOmate keeps to it: what your policy keeps on your computer is only ever read by the local model, and connectors are held to the same rules.

  • Next

    One memory for the whole team

    Everyone works from the same rules, funders and decisions. Keep it on storage you run, with the sign-in you already use, or let us host it.

  • Next

    Tasks that come with your tools

    A connected service can bring its own ready-made tasks, so new skills arrive without an update.

  • Later

    A model trained for NGO work

    An open-weight model tuned for funding calls, deadlines, eligibility and reporting, in the languages our users work in. Trained on reviewed and donated examples, never on anyone's private work, and published with its weights.

  • Later

    An open benchmark for the sector

    Real NGO tasks, public results. So anyone can see which models actually do this work, and which ones make things up.

  • Later

    With you in the field

    A mobile app for teams on the move, and NGOmate built into the NGOstack desktop app.

The app stays free and open source, whatever comes next. This is where we are heading, not what ships today; follow along or help shape it on GitHub.

Check what it writes

Local models make mistakes, including mixing up real figures. NGOmate flags dates and links it cannot trace to your material or a tool, but you still need to check names and numbers before you use a draft.

Built on open standards

Nothing in NGOmate ties you to us. Every part is something you can inspect, replace or take with you.

Open-weight models
Qwen, Ministral and Granite, in the common GGUF format, downloaded from Hugging Face and pinned to an exact version.
llama.cpp
The open llama.cpp server runs the model on your computer. NGOmate also works with any model server that speaks the chat completions format, such as Ollama.
Model Context Protocol
Connectors use the open MCP standard, so any service that offers an MCP server can be connected, not only ones we chose.
Plain files
Memory is Markdown in a folder on your computer. You can read it in any text editor, back it up, or move it elsewhere.
Your system's keychain
Access tokens for connectors are kept in the operating system's own keychain.
Open source
The app is built with Tauri, and all of NGOmate's code is public under the MIT licence.

Where it stands

NGOmate is an early preview: chat, drafting from your documents, memory, connectors and the three ready-made tasks work, and much of what is above is still to come.

The installers are not signed yet, so your computer will warn you the first time. On a Mac, open it once, then choose Open Anyway under System Settings → Privacy & Security. On Windows, choose More info → Run anyway.

If you work in a small organization, tell us which tasks eat your week, as an issue on GitHub. That is how the next ready-made tasks get chosen.
